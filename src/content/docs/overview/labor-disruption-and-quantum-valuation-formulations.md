Disruption claims evaluate productivity loss and economic inefficiency within discrete activities, differing fundamentally from delay claims, which focus on critical path impacts<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. Disrupted work often proceeds without delaying project completion dates, but it causes significant financial loss through inefficient resource consumption<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. Calculating disruption requires proving two legal elements: factual causation (the specific employer-risk event that degraded productivity) and the resulting quantum of financial loss<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>.  
The Society of Construction Law Protocol and AACE Recommended Practice 25R-03 establish the Measured Mile as the preferred methodology for quantifying disruption<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. The Measured Mile compares a contractor's actual labor productivity during an unhindered baseline period (the "clean" mile) against productivity on identical work during a period impacted by disruptive events<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. By comparing a contractor’s own historical performance on the same project, this method insulates the claim from employer arguments alleging inaccurate original tender estimates or general labor inefficiency<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>.  
When records are insufficient to support a Measured Mile, contractors often resort to industry-published factor studies (such as MCAA or CII factor curves) or global Modified Total Cost claims<sup>[11](/references/#ref-11)</sup>. However, domestic courts and international arbitration panels treat these theoretical and global methods with skepticism, requiring claimants to demonstrate that their original tender rates were realistic, actual costs were prudently incurred, and no contractor inefficiencies contributed to the cost variance<sup>[11](/references/#ref-11)</sup>.  
For compensable delay, contractors are entitled to recover extended site preliminaries alongside unabsorbed Head Office Overhead (HOOH) and lost profit opportunities<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. Three primary mathematical formulas govern head office overhead recovery across international practice: the Hudson, Emden, and Eichleay formulations<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>.

| Formulation / Method | Mathematical Structure | Operational Purpose and Basis | Judicial Acceptance and Limitations |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Measured Mile Analysis** (AACE 25R-03 / SCL)<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup> | $\text{Disruption Hours} = (\mathit{Prod}_{\text{Impacted}} - \mathit{Prod}_{\text{Baseline}}) \times \text{Quantity}_{\text{Impacted}}$ | Quantifies lost labor and equipment productivity by comparing disrupted periods with unhindered baseline periods on the same site<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. | The benchmark standard in international construction arbitration; requires granular, contemporaneous daily records<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. |
| **Modified Total Cost Method**10 | $\text{Claim} = C_{\text{Actual}} - C_{\text{Tender}} - C_{\text{Contractor-caused}}$ | Calculates financial loss by adjusting total project cost overruns to account for bid errors and contractor-caused issues. | Highly restricted; accepted only when individual cause-and-effect pairs cannot be isolated despite detailed analysis<sup>[11](/references/#ref-11)</sup>. |
| **Hudson Formula (HOOH)**12 | $\text{Claim} = \left(\frac{HO\% + P\%}{100}\right) \times \frac{\text{Contract Sum}}{\text{Contract Period}} \times \text{Period of Delay}$ | Calculates unabsorbed head office overhead and lost profit resulting from extended project performance<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. | Widely cited in common-law jurisdictions, but criticized for using theoretical tender percentages rather than actual overhead losses<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. |
| **Emden Formula (HOOH)**12 | $\text{Claim} = \left(\frac{\text{Actual Total Enterprise Overhead \& Profit \%}}{100}\right) \times \frac{\text{Contract Sum}}{\text{Contract Period}} \times \text{Period of Delay}$ | Refines the Hudson formula by substituting the actual enterprise overhead percentage for the tender estimate<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. | Preferred over Hudson in UK tribunals because it derives overhead rates from validated company financial accounts<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. |
| **Eichleay Formula (HOOH)**12 | $\text{Allocable Overhead} = \frac{\text{Project Billings}}{\text{Total Billings}} \times \text{Total Overhead}$ $\text{Daily Overhead Rate} = \frac{\text{Allocable Overhead}}{\text{Days of Performance}}$ $\text{Claim} = \text{Daily Overhead Rate} \times \text{Days of Delay}$ | Allocates enterprise head office overhead based on the ratio of contract billings to total company revenue during the contract window<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. | Mandatory in US federal public contracting; internationally recognized when the contractor proves it was placed on standby<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>. |
