# **Table of Contents: Contracts and Commercial Management Body of Knowledge for the Construction Industry (CCM-BOK)**


The commercial management of capital projects has historically suffered from functional fragmentation. Conventional project management disciplines have frequently treated commercial and contractual administration as secondary, transactional support functions subordinate to physical engineering and site execution. However, escalating capital project scale, systemic supply-chain volatility, hyper-specialized procurement interfaces, and stringent regulatory safety and decarbonization mandates require an integrated, authoritative professional framework<sup>[1](/references/#ref-1)</sup>.  
The Contracts and Commercial Management Body of Knowledge for the Construction Industry (CCM-BOK) reconciles and unifies the standards of leading professional bodies, integrating the core competencies of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS)<sup>[1](/references/#ref-1)</sup>, the National Contract Management Association (NCMA)<sup>[2](/references/#ref-2)</sup>, World Commerce & Contracting (WorldCC, formerly IACCM)<sup>[6](/references/#ref-6)</sup>, the Construction Management Association of America (CMAA)<sup>[8](/references/#ref-8)</sup>, the Association for the Advancement of Cost Engineering International (AACE)<sup>[10](/references/#ref-10)</sup>, the Society of Construction Law (SCL)<sup>[12](/references/#ref-12)</sup>, and the International Organization for Standardization (ISO)<sup>[14](/references/#ref-14)</sup>.

The structural organization of the CCM-BOK is configured across seven primary operational volumes:

* Volume I covers foundational concepts, legal doctrines, statutory frameworks, and standard contract architectures<sup>[2](/references/#ref-2)</sup>.  
* Volume II details the upstream capital procurement lifecycle, delivery system selection, pricing structures, estimating, tendering, and contract formation<sup>[2](/references/#ref-2)</sup>.  
* Volume III governs downstream contract administration, scheduling baselines, site inspection records, valuation regimes, payment mechanisms, and change control<sup>[11](/references/#ref-11)</sup>.  
* Volume IV addresses internal project commercial controls, Cost Value Reconciliation (CVR), earned value management, supply chain governance, subcontract packaging, and risk allocation<sup>[1](/references/#ref-1)</sup>.  
* Volume V establishes the technical and legal requirements for claims engineering, schedule delay analysis, labor disruption calculation, quantum substantiation, and tiered dispute resolution<sup>[10](/references/#ref-10)</sup>.  
* Volume VI incorporates contemporary and emerging disciplines, spanning Building Information Modeling (BIM), smart contracts, PAS 2080 carbon governance, and building safety statutory dutyholder regimes<sup>[19](/references/#ref-19)</sup>.  
* Volume VII provides a compendium of forensic delay equations, multi-standard competency matrices, governance registers, and standard operating toolkits<sup>[2](/references/#ref-2)</sup>.
