The selection of a standard form of contract governs the operational architecture, risk profile, and administrative duties of project commercial teams<sup>[16](/references/#ref-16)</sup>. The global construction marketplace is dominated by four standard suites: FIDIC, NEC4, JCT, and CMAA25. Each suite approaches programmatic risk, change control, and collaborative duties through fundamentally distinct commercial philosophies.  
FIDIC standard forms—traditionally exemplified by the Red, Yellow, and Silver Books—prioritize an explicit, tripartite balance in which an independent Engineer acts as the administrative authority and adjudicator of first instance under Sub-Clause 3.738. The 2017 updates, reinforced by the 2022 reprints, introduced heightened administrative formalization<sup>[38](/references/#ref-38)</sup>. They established strict 28-day notice-of-claim time-bars for both Employer and Contractor, separated disputes from ordinary claims, and mandated standing Dispute Avoidance/Adjudication Boards (DAABs) to facilitate real-time conflict mitigation rather than retrospective arbitration<sup>[38](/references/#ref-38)</sup>.  
Conversely, the NEC4 suite approaches capital delivery from a collaborative, process-driven project management perspective<sup>[37](/references/#ref-37)</sup>. Under the NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract (ECC), commercial obligations are directly tied to scheduling integrity<sup>[37](/references/#ref-37)</sup>. Clause 31 requires the regular submission and formal acceptance of a living programme, which becomes the sole baseline for assessing changes<sup>[37](/references/#ref-37)</sup>. Rather than treating claims and variations under separate contractual mechanisms, NEC4 integrates time and cost into a single, prospective change regime known as the Compensation Event (Clause 60–65)<sup>[37](/references/#ref-37)</sup>. Strict early warning notifications (Clause 15\) and eight-week contractor notification time-bars enforce real-time commercial settlement, actively penalizing parties who defer quantum or schedule assessments until project completion<sup>[37](/references/#ref-37)</sup>.  
The JCT suite, substantially modernized in its 2024 edition, maintains the traditional Anglo-Welsh procedural architecture while introducing structural modifications driven by the UK Construction Playbook and market developments<sup>[35](/references/#ref-35)</sup>. The 2024 revisions eliminated the optional status of collaborative working, dispute notification, and environmental sustainability clauses, converting them into mandatory operative terms<sup>[35](/references/#ref-35)</sup>. Furthermore, JCT 2024 modified the extension of time regime by shortening the Employer's determination period from 12 weeks to 8 weeks, imposing a 14-day limit on requests for further information, and incorporating the legal principles of the UK Supreme Court decision in *Triple Point v PTT* to clarify that liquidated damages accrue up to the exact date of termination, after which general damages apply<sup>[35](/references/#ref-35)</sup>.  
In the North American sector, the CMAA standards of practice govern Agency Construction Management and Construction Management-at-Risk (CMAR) delivery systems<sup>[8](/references/#ref-8)</sup>. Under the CMAR framework, the commercial entity shifts during the project lifecycle from an owner-aligned pre-construction professional advisor into a delivery contractor holding trade package agreements under a Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP)<sup>[25](/references/#ref-25)</sup>. Change administration is managed through formal Change Orders and Construction Change Directives, where cost and schedule adjustments are evaluated against a defined contingency allowance shared between the owner and the CM25.
